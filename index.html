About the course

Digital Platforms Test Drive

A hands-on course in ESSEC's MSc in Marketing Management & Digital, taught by Madina Kalyayeva. It covers ad buying, audience targeting, influencer selection, content trends, analytics, and AI for content production and project management.

Sessions are co-led by the platforms themselves and by agencies and partners, who show the tools marketers use to get a campaign seen in a crowded feed. Platforms change fast, so the course is a foundation for keeping up rather than a fixed manual.

Ten platform types and the numbers behind them

The opening session mapped the ten types of digital marketing platform, each with a Southeast Asian example, and read the DataReportal and GWI figures on how people actually use social media. It closed on campaigns, channel management, data collection and the eight families of benchmarking metrics.

Brands used to choose audiences by age, location and interests. Now Meta Advantage+, Google Performance Max and TikTok Smart+ find the audience, so the creative does the targeting.

The AI shift

  • Search becomes answers. AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity answer before anyone clicks, so brands compete to be cited.
  • Targeting becomes AI buying. Marketers set goals, data and creative; the platform picks audience, placement and budget.
  • Production becomes GenAI. One idea turns into hundreds of ad versions in days.
  • Shopping goes social and agentic. TikTok Shop, Shopee Live, and AI agents that check out for you.
  • New ad space. Ads in Google AI Overviews (SG, Dec 2025) and ChatGPT Ads (SG, 23 Sep 2026).
  • Trust is the currency. Label AI content and protect authenticity.

Key definitions

  • Social media campaign: a planned series of content over a set period, aimed at an outcome that can be tracked.
  • Social media strategy: the goals and tactics across channels, campaigns and media placements.
  • Channel management: backing the best channel for each audience and goal. Prioritise, evaluate the tech, then measure and tweak.
  • Ad campaigns: placing ads and investing in channels for better ROI. On a tight budget, run fewer, bigger ads and repurpose video across formats.

Data collection

The struggles: limited first-party data, poor third-party data, hard segmentation and privacy rules.

  • Build first-party data with core consumers and key media partners.
  • Keep collection compliant by working with legal and partnerships.
  • Reach people without tracking through contextual targeting and data clean rooms.
  • Let customers decide what they share, and allow opt-out.

Benchmarking

Measuring a multichannel approach is hard because there is no common set of metrics. The two most used are transactional (sales, revenue, conversion) and competitive (market, voice and wallet share).

Then come engagement, perceptual, pipeline, relationship and awareness, plus a new family: AI visibility, how often AI answers mention your brand.

Reflection

The big idea

Every big platform now buys the audience for you, so the creative does the targeting. The skill shifts from choosing people to feeding the AI the right signals.

For our project

Choose platforms by what our audience actually does there, using the DataReportal figures, and add AI visibility to our KPIs.

The martech stack and the case for paid media

Session 2 walked through Gartner's transit map and the 2026 martech landscape of 15,505 products, then sorted tools into point tools, platforms, suites and hubs, with AI copilots and agents as a new layer on top. Organic posts now reach roughly 3–5% of Instagram followers and about 1% on Facebook. Paid media buys reach, but only for as long as it is funded.

Session notes

  • Stations are provider or product categories.

The point: no single provider is best in class at everything, so marketers combine tools.

Organic vs paid

Organic is unpaid content that builds relationships, brand voice and community: regular posts, employee advocacy, replies, shared UGC.

Session 2 · 9 Oct 2026 · Guest workshop · LaunchLab (Yibo, Nirvan, Rishabh)

Design, build, deploy

The first guest session showed how to ship a working website without hand-coding it: shape the look in Claude Design, hand it off to Claude Code to build, store it on GitHub and publish it with Vercel. It ended with Supabase for log-ins and data, and with this very assignment.

A vague prompt gets a vague site. Give the model a role, a goal, an audience, the pages, a style, the features and the output you expect.

Session notes

The whole workflow

Claude Design→Handoff to Claude Code→Build the files→Push to GitHub→Deploy on Vercel

Design the look in Claude Design without writing code. Build by sending the design to Claude Code through Share, then “Handoff to Claude Code…”. Deploy on localhost or tiiny.host for a quick start, or through GitHub and Vercel for a site that updates itself.

The art of prompting

A bad prompt:

Create a website for my course.

A good prompt states a role, a goal, the target audience, the core pages, the design style, the key features and the output you want back, and asks for a build that can be expanded later.

What Claude Code does

An agentic coding system that reads a whole codebase, runs commands and debugs failures.

  • Routines: repeated background jobs, such as checking a folder for errors every hour.
  • Refactoring: rename or tidy code without changing what it does.
  • Session context: remembers commands run, earlier errors, changed files and the goal.

GitHub

Stores the project and its history (the repository), shares it, and lets a team work without overwriting each other.

  • Push sends commits straight up, with no review. Use it solo or on your own branch.
  • Pull request proposes changes for teammates to review before merging.

Vercel

GitHub stores the code; Vercel turns it into a website.

  • Sign in with GitHub and import the repo once.
  • Every push rebuilds the live site in seconds.
  • Every pull request gets its own preview link.

Three files to know

.html
the words and structure people read
.css
the look: colours, fonts, spacing, phone vs laptop
.js
the behaviour: clicks, typing, submit

Beyond a static site

A plain site forgets every visitor and knows only its own files. Remembering users needs a backend.

  • Sign up adds a row with your email and a scrambled password.
  • Log in checks them and hands the browser a token.
  • Log out throws the token away.

Supabase provides log-in and a database, and connects to GitHub, Vercel and Lovable.

The assignment

Build a website with a tab for each guest speaker session this semester, each with an interactive game to consolidate what was learned, using the tools from this workshop. This site is that assignment.

Reflection

The big idea

A prompt works like a brief: role, goal, audience, pages, style, features and output. Vague in, vague out.

For our project

We can mock up campaign pages the same way, design then build then deploy, and label them as AI-assisted.

Session 3 · 23 Oct 2026 · Lecture

Media buying and the objective that steers it

Session 3 made the case for paid media and for the agencies that buy it, then broke down Meta's six campaign objectives stage by stage of the funnel. It closed by launching the final group project: an omni-channel campaign with a $100,000 simulated budget and at least one AI component.

The objective is the GPS. It decides how the algorithm optimises delivery, who sees the ad, and how success is counted.

Session notes

Paid vs organic, side by side

Speed & scale
Paid: immediate reach and traffic. Organic: slower growth that needs consistent engagement.
Targeting
Paid: precision by demographics, interests and behaviour. Organic: limited to followers and the algorithm.
Visibility
Paid: guaranteed, unaffected by algorithm changes. Organic: subject to the algorithm, unpredictable reach.
Conversion
Paid: optimised for sales, leads and visits. Organic: builds the brand story, fewer direct sales.
Competition
Paid: keeps the brand visible against rivals. Organic: easily drowned out by brands with budgets.
Cost
Paid: needs ongoing budget. Organic: free, but takes long-term effort.

The winning agency approach runs both: organic builds trust and community, paid reaches new audiences and boosts the organic posts that already work.

The money in paid social

US$339B
worldwide social ad spend projected for 2026 (Statista)
US$588M
Singapore social ad spend in 2025, up 15.2%, about a fifth of all ad spend
US$119M
Singapore influencer marketing
+8.3%
Asia-Pacific digital ad spend growth in 2026 (eMarketer)

The catch with paid

  • Cost: a small budget can't sustain it for long.
  • Short-term: results stop when the spend stops.
  • Authenticity: paid exposure builds less genuine connection.
  • Black box: AI automation decides where the money goes, so you need your own measurement.

Why AI still needs a strategist

  • Context: AI may raise budget because engagement spiked; a human checks what caused the spike.
  • Data: bad data gives bad recommendations.
  • Whose metric: platform AI optimises the platform's numbers; humans check what drove incremental sales.
  • Creativity: stories, emotion and cultural nuance are still human strengths.

Media agencies in 2026

  • Omnicom completed its IPG acquisition in Nov 2025, about US$26B in revenue.
  • GroupM became WPP Media in May 2025.
  • Agency AI platforms: WPP Open, Publicis Marcel, Omnicom Omni, dentsu.Connect, Havas Converged.AI.
  • As platforms automate the buy, agencies shift to strategy, data, creative and measurement.
  • ChatGPT Ads in Southeast Asia are sold through the big holding companies as well as self-serve.

Meta's six objectives, down the funnel

Awareness · top
Brand awareness (people likely to remember you) or Reach (as many people as possible). A jewellery house launching a collection.
Traffic · middle
Link clicks, landing page views or Messenger. A retailer driving shoppers to its sale.
Engagement · middle
Post engagement, page likes, event responses, video views. An influencer pushing shares of a workout video.
Leads · middle
Instant forms, Messenger leads or website conversions. A skincare brand collecting enquiries.
App promotion · bottom
Installs, in-app events and retention. A mobile-first or subscription app.
Sales · bottom
Conversions, catalogue sales and store traffic. A multi-brand store retargeting past buyers.

Optimise, and avoid the usual mistakes

  • Test several creatives, audiences and CTAs, and A/B test them.
  • Watch CTR, CPA and ROAS, and move budget and placements on performance.
  • Match the conversion window to the sales cycle.
  • Common mistakes: the wrong objective, ignoring data, not optimising, no custom or lookalike audiences, no clear CTA.

AI toolkit 2026, by job

  • Research: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity
  • Images: Gemini, Midjourney, Firefly, Canva, Mujo.ai
  • Video & voice: Veo, Runway, Kling, Sora, HeyGen, ElevenLabs
  • Platform-native: Advantage+ creative, Symphony, Asset Studio
  • Listening: Brand24, Meltwater, Brandwatch
  • Agents: Zapier, n8n, ManyChat, Meta Business Agent

Paid media is rented attention, and the objective is the instruction you give the algorithm. Pick the wrong one and the platform will find the wrong people very efficiently.

Session 4 · 30 Oct 2026 · Lecture

Managing campaigns, starting from the brief

Session 4 walked through who does what in a media team and how AI is changing each role, then spent most of its time on the most important document in a campaign: the brief. An agency proposal for WWF Singapore's workplace programme showed a brief turned into insight, creative and a 12-week media plan.

If the brief is unclear, don't guess. Send a reverse brief. AI can draft and stress-test it, but the objectives, budget and big idea stay with people.

Session notes

The media team, then and now

Strategist
Sets the strategy, channels, budgets and tactics. Now uses AI for research and scenarios but still owns the objective and big idea.
Buyer
Negotiates and buys placements for the best value and reach. Now sets goals, data and guardrails for AI buying tools.
Trader
Runs programmatic buying, optimising bids and placements in real time. Less manual bidding than before.
Analyst
Tracks performance and recommends optimisations. Now moving to incrementality tests and mix models such as Google Meridian and Meta Robyn.
Manager
Keeps timelines, budgets, deliverables and stakeholders on track. Now adds AI governance: use, disclosure, brand safety, privacy.
New roles
Creative-automation specialists, data engineers and measurement leads. Postings asking for “AI agent” skills grew 1,587% in 2025.

From brief to plan

Brief received→Brief dissection→Brief questioning→Discovery call→Media planning

Then: research & insights on audience, market and competitors, strategy development on channels, budgets and journey, and execution, optimisation & measurement once the campaign is live.

Four core components

  • Goals: the broader outcome, such as brand awareness or sales.
  • Target audience: who the campaign is for.
  • Value proposition: why they'd choose this over a competitor.
  • Metrics: the KPIs that track success.

Question the brief

Check it is complete (budget, dates, assets), reliable (where did that data come from?) and actionable.

On the sample Netflix brief: What's the end date? Is $1.5M all media, and in what currency? Why only Gen Z? Are impressions the right metric for subscriber growth?

The brief in the AI era

  • Add: first-party data and conversion signals, how much creative the AI needs to test, and the measurement plan.
  • AI rules: where AI may be used, disclosure, approvals, brand safety, privacy (Singapore's PDPA).
  • New touchpoints: ChatGPT Ads (SG, Sep 2026) and AI Overviews ads (SG, Dec 2025).
  • Stress-test: ask AI for gaps, then confirm with the client.

Case: WWF Singapore, Together Possible @ Work

The brief: show what makes working with WWF unique, the benefits of employee engagement, and shift workplace giving in Singapore. The audience: productivity-driven senior professionals aged 30–50 who own culture and wellbeing.

Insight 1
Employees put happiness, purpose and wellbeing over money. 44% would earn less to contribute to society.
Insight 2
CSR and team building feel extracurricular. Only 1 in 10 companies called CSR a top priority.
Shift
From pleasure-driven team bonding to purpose-driven engagement that helps employees and the planet.
Creative line
“Purpose is ours to create”, under the direction “Together we lead with purpose”.
Programmes
Together We Move (team building), Together We Give (workplace giving), Together We Raise (fundraising).
Content
Organic: testimonials, workshop sneak peeks, social impact. Paid: thought leadership, USP and lead-gen catalogue downloads.

Building an AI marketing team

Nick Goh, CMO of Yellow Pages Singapore, marketing lead of YP Academy and CEO of INSPIRO Consulting, argued that AI in marketing is a people problem before it is a tool problem. He then built a working “AI marketing team” live in Claude, taking a fictional luxury handbag launch from an emailed brief to scored creative.

The AI earned the most trust when it said “to confirm”, flagged a target as unrealistic, or refused a request. Humans own every handoff.

The new team shape

Session 5 · 13 Nov 2026 · Lecture

Audience insights and where to invest

Session 5 followed a campaign from brief to launch, separated media strategy from the media plan, and laid out the six stages of planning. Most of it was about knowing the audience: qualitative insight versus quantitative size, the tools for each, what AI changes, and why not every customer is worth the same.

Size the market with research tools, size the reachable audience with platform tools, then let the AI find the people inside it.

Session notes

Six stages of media planning

Business context→Audience insights→Channel decisions→Investment decisions→Measurement plan→Media plan

Media strategy is the high-level how, where and when. The media plan is the tactical detail: channels, budget split and timing. Planning is also about giving stakeholders confidence through well-founded decisions.

A planner's challenges

  • A constantly changing media landscape
  • Budget constraints and stakeholder priorities
  • Finding the audience
  • Platform and channel fragmentation
  • Attribution and measurement

Insight vs size

Audience insights are qualitative: who they are and why they act. Use research tools (GWI, Nielsen) and site analytics (GA4).

Audience size is quantitative: how many you can reach. Use the platforms where you'll buy (Meta, DV360, TikTok). Getting both right cuts media wastage, which is ads shown to people outside the target.

Tools, paid and free

  • Subscription: GWI, Nielsen, Semrush, Meltwater
  • Site analytics: Google Analytics 4, Adobe Analytics
  • Free: Google Trends, Think with Google, TikTok, Meta, Reddit and AI assistants
  • Sizing: Google Keyword Planner, TikTok Audience Insights, LinkedIn forecasting, Meta audience tools

Questions that unlock insight

  • What are their online behaviours, and which channels do they use?
  • How do they research products, and what drives the purchase?
  • What content do they consume, and what are they interested in?
  • How do they feel about brands? Are there case studies of similar campaigns?

AI for audience insights

Deep research agents
ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity draft an audience brief in minutes. Always check the sources they cite.
AI inside the tools
GWI Spark, Meltwater Mira and Nielsen Ad Intel AI answer questions in plain language.
Renamed
Facebook IQ is now Meta Foresight; Semrush is now part of Adobe.
Synthetic audiences
AI “twins” answer surveys in hours. Aveeno cut a strategy brief from 30 days to 3.
But check the proof
Pew found AI twins flattened opinion (98% vs 42% on one question). Strat7 found willingness-to-pay 16% too high.
How to use it
Explore and form hypotheses with AI, validate with real data, and cap synthetic respondents at 10–20% of a sample.

Audience sizing in the AI era

Meta
With Advantage+ audience, age and interests are only suggestions. Detailed-targeting exclusions were removed in 2025.
LinkedIn
Location is required and comes first. Minimum 300 members to serve, 50,000+ recommended.
TikTok
Audience Insights counts accounts that saw ads in the last 30 days, not people, and no under-18s.
Google
Keyword Planner forecasts at campaign level by city and device; Reach Planner adds weekly frequency benchmarks.

Four investment principles

  • Annual ROI: judge spend over 12 months, not one burst.
  • Customer quality: a customer worth many repeat purchases can justify a higher acquisition cost.
  • Design to learn: build tests into the plan.
  • Long-term incrementality: fund what truly adds sales.

gets high value from the brand and gives high value back
Vulnerable
very valuable to the brand, but gets little value in return
Free rider
loves the offers and samples, but isn't very profitable
Lost cause
gets little value and gives little profit

Build notes

How this site was built

The site follows the workflow from the LaunchLab workshop, using the tools shown in class, and grows one stop at a time as sessions run.

Design

Started from a structured prompt (role, goal, audience, pages, style, features, output) and gave the site one idea: the course is a test drive. Road signs set the type and colour, the tab bar is a road, and your score is a driving licence.

Build

One self-contained page built with Claude. Four game engines (sort, higher-or-lower, order and build) are fed by question lists written from each session's slides, so adding a session means adding data, not new code.

Deploy

A single index.html, ready to push to a GitHub repository and import into Vercel, which redeploys on every push. Dropping it into tiiny.host works too.
